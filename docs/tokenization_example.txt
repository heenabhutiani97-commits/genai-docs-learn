## TOKENIZATION -- PRACTICAL EXAMPLE:

Let's take one simple input:

"I am learning GENAI"

This is our input/prompt.

Tokenizer will break this input into smaller chunks, and these smaller chunks are called tokens.

For example, it can be broken like:

"I" , " am" , " learning" , " GEN" , "AI"

Here we can see that token is not always equal to a complete word. One word can also be divided into multiple tokens depending on the tokenizer.

After creating tokens, every token is mapped to a unique numerical ID called Token ID.

For example:

"I"          --> 40
" am"        --> 716
" learning"  --> 4201
" GEN"       --> 9832
"AI"         --> 1587

These numbers are just examples.

So the complete flow is:

Input
↓
Tokenizer
↓
Tokens
↓
Token IDs
↓
LLM

Important point:

Token = piece of text

Token ID = number representing that token

LLM needs numbers instead of raw text because the model performs mathematical/statistical computations on numerical representations.

---

## ANOTHER EXAMPLE:

Input:

"I love working with data"

Tokenizer can break it into smaller pieces like:

"I" , " love" , " working" , " with" , " data"

Then these tokens are mapped to Token IDs.

So:

"I love working with data"
↓
Tokenizer
↓
["I", " love", " working", " with", " data"]
↓
Token IDs
↓
LLM

The important thing to understand here is that the LLM does not directly take our raw text and process it as humans understand it.

The text first goes through tokenization and gets converted into numerical representations which the model can process.
